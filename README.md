## 🔬 Research Profile

My doctoral dissertation (2019–2025) focuses on macroeconomics and firms’ access to finance, particularly the relationship between government borrowing and corporate financing decisions.

Beginning in 2018, I collected annual reports and financial statements for **1,802 publicly listed and privately held firms across 29 African countries**. Using web scraping techniques, followed by manual verification, I constructed a comprehensive firm-year panel dataset.

By integrating these firm-level observations with macroeconomic data, I examined how domestic and external public debt affect corporate borrowing, capital structure, and financial conditions across African countries. The dataset and accompanying code are publicly available on my personal website ([www.theogenehabimana.com](https://www.theogenehabimana.com)).

My current research includes cross-country studies of government debt and corporate capital structure, as well as a collaborative research project with researchers at Harvard University examining sovereign debt in Sub-Saharan Africa, supported by the Center for the Study of African Societies and Economies (CSASE) at Harvard University.

My ongoing research also includes a project funded by the **Foundation for Economic Education (LSR 2025, Application No. 32-16490)**, which investigates the role of artificial intelligence (AI) and cybersecurity risks in corporate financing decisions using machine learning and computational text analysis.

As part of this project, I have developed firm-level measures of AI adoption and cybersecurity risk by systematically extracting and analyzing information from corporate annual reports of companies across **91 countries**.

These measures provide a foundation for examining how emerging technologies and cybersecurity-related risks influence firms’ financing decisions and capital structures in both developed and emerging markets.

## 🧪 Current Research Projects

### 1. Sovereign Debt in Sub-Saharan Africa Project (Ongoing)

**Research collaborator** on the project sponsored by the Center for the Study of African Societies and Economies (CSASE) at Harvard University.

The project examines sovereign debt accumulation, fiscal sustainability, public debt management, and the economic consequences of government borrowing in Sub-Saharan African countries.

My research interests within this collaboration include the relationship between sovereign borrowing, private-sector credit, and corporate financing.

### 2. Rethinking Capital Structure Theories in the Age of Rising Public Debt: Crowding-Out versus Crowding-In (Ongoing)

Empirical analysis of government debt and corporate leverage using a global panel covering approximately **100 countries and more than 55,000 firms over 1990–2024**.

The research examines the effects of domestic and external government borrowing on firms’ financing decisions, with attention to institutional quality, financial development, and differences across countries.

### 3. Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity Risk, and Corporate Financing (Ongoing Research)

A research project funded by the Foundation for Economic Education (LSR 2025, Application No. 32-16490).

The project investigates the role of artificial intelligence (AI) and cybersecurity risks in corporate financing decisions using machine learning and computational text analysis.

As part of this research, I have developed firm-level measures of AI adoption and cybersecurity risk through systematic extraction and analysis of corporate annual reports across **91 countries**.

The research also examines cybersecurity risk, artificial intelligence, ownership concentration, and corporate financing.
